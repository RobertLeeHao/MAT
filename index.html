05How it worksAnswers A · D

Start to finish, in one chat.

  1. 01

    Ask in one chat.

    Ask MAT in plain words, in the personality you pick: calm, doting or grumpy. A question gets its answer right there; a job gets a plan, with its time and cost next to one strong model’s.

  2. 02

    MAT staffs it — or doesn’t.

    One model when one is enough. When the work splits, MAT puts a crew on cheaper models, side by side, in a drawer beside the chat. Every member has its own face and watches its work: you see at a glance who’s working, who’s done and who’s waiting. Only MAT looks at you.

  3. 03

    It asks only when it must.

    Every stage passes MAT’s check. When a decision is yours, MAT asks, and the crew waits for you.

  4. 04

    You get the result.

    MAT signs off the last stage and hands you the work, with its sources, the time it took and what it cost.

10Questions

Questions, answered.

Q 01

Is MAT another AI model?

No.

MAT runs on the models you already have — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and others, plus models running on your own computer. It decides which one does each step, and checks the work.

Q 02

Which subscriptions can I connect?

The ones you have.

Your ChatGPT plan, API keys from the major providers — Claude, OpenAI, Gemini, DeepSeek, Kimi, Qwen and more — and models running on your own computer through Ollama.

Q 03

Is it always cheaper than one model?

No.

On a small job, one model is cheaper and faster, and MAT says so and runs it that way. The bigger the job, the more a crew of cheaper models saves.

Q 04

Do I have to manage the crew?

Not yet.

For now MAT staffs every crew for you: you talk to MAT, in one chat, and it asks only when a decision is yours. Next, you’ll be able to build crews of your own — the seats, their models and the checks — and share them (Coming soon · 03).

Q 05

When can I use it?

Now.

Download the beta for Mac (macOS 13 or later) — it’s free while in beta, and runs on the models you bring. Ideas, questions and bugs go to GitHub Discussions.

B·00Get the beta

Download it. Open it once from Settings.

The beta isn’t notarized by Apple yet, so the first time, macOS asks you to confirm that you want to open it. After that it opens like any app.

  1. Download and unzip.

    MAT-macOS.zip, from GitHub. Double-click it, then drag MAT into Applications. macOS 13 or later, on Apple silicon or Intel.

  2. Open MAT once.

    macOS says it can’t check MAT for malicious software. Click Done — this is the expected first step for an app that isn’t notarized.

  3. Allow it in System Settings.

    Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll to Security and click Open Anyway next to MAT, then enter your password. The button is there for about an hour after you tried to open it.

  4. Connect your models.

    In MAT, Settings › Providers › Add provider…: a Claude or OpenAI key, a ChatGPT plan, DeepSeek, Gemini, or Ollama on your own computer. Your keys stay on your computer.

Updates
New builds come out as GitHub releases. Download the new zip and replace MAT in Applications; your chats, memory and keys stay. The first time a new build reads your keys, macOS may ask once — click Always Allow.
Something broke
Tell us in Discussions, or open a bug with your MAT version (Settings › About). Never paste a key.
Removing it
Settings › Data › Erase everything, then drag MAT to the Trash.

B·01Changelog

What changed in the beta.

MAT is in an open beta. Each build is a release on GitHub; this is what each one changed.

  1. Oct 19, 2026

    0.1.5: see where every model comes from.

    • Settings › Providers is one list: subscriptions, keys and the models on your Mac are the same kind of row, top to bottom in the order MAT tries them. Drag a key to change the order.
    • Each connection is tested every time the page opens, and the row says what came back: working and how fast, rate limited, out of credit, or key refused.
    • What MAT uses, at the top of the page: the model each size of work reaches for first, through which connection, and how many it falls back to. MAT picks; you can pin one.
    • How much of each allowance is used is drawn in the provider’s colour, behind its row and in the sidebar. A provider that works but reports no balance is striped, and MAT tells you what it spent there itself.
    • Claude Code and Codex appear as soon as they’re installed, with their sign-in state. A signed-in Claude Code is no longer reported as signed out.
  2. Oct 19, 2026

    0.1.4: plans you can steer, methods that keep themselves.

    • Before a job runs, MAT shows its plan with three numbers you can set: cost, time and quality. A one-line summary says what you set, and each stage says how it flows: in order, one each, a few versions, send back, or ask you first.
    • When something could be a quick write or a planned job, MAT asks which, with time, price and quality on both sides. A job can also run tonight, or be done by a date.
    • What MAT learns from a job is kept as one method per kind of work. A method is updated rather than duplicated, scored by how the jobs that used it went, and put away after 60 days unused. Settings › Memory shows the record.
    • The judge that answers MAT’s yes-or-no questions is now your choice: Jev, a small model on your Mac through Ollama (one click to install, in setup or in Settings), or your cheapest chat model.
    • Your Claude Pro / Max plan or ChatGPT plan can do the work through Claude Code or Codex, with no bill per call. When the plan’s limit is hit, MAT moves to your other providers and comes back when it resets.
  3. Oct 15, 2026

    0.1.3: tools, and you stay in charge.

    • Settings › Tools connects the apps and data a seat can use, over MCP. Pick from MAT’s list — each one says up front whether it works today, needs a key or an admin first, or can’t be used yet — paste a link or a command, or bring over what you set up in Claude, Cursor or VS Code.
    • A tool that only reads runs on its own. A tool that changes something asks you first, in the chat and in plain words; only that seat waits.
    • Tools on your Mac run in a sandbox: they see the folders you gave them, never your keys, ~/.ssh or your browsers. A tool whose description changes after you allowed it stays off until you look.
    • Once a job has read both your own data and something from outside, anything that would send it out asks you first, even when the tool is set to Allow.
    • Notifications when a job needs you or is done, and scheduled tasks that run on their own. With no model connected, MAT suggests one that fits your Mac and sets it up through Ollama.
    • At its budget or time limit, a job asks whether to carry on instead of stopping halfway. Click a seat to see what it was told and what it did; Settings › Memory can be searched and edited.
  4. Oct 14, 2026

    0.1.2: more accounts, files in the chat.

    • Add the same provider more than once, or any OpenAI-compatible server. When one account is out of quota or rate-limited, MAT moves on to the next.
    • Send files and screenshots; pictures go to the models that can see them. What MAT delivers opens, downloads or opens with another app right from the chat.
    • Each seat gets this week’s cheapest model that clears the quality its step needs. Models shows the week.
    • MAT writes each seat’s whole instruction, and the sign-off fits the kind of work. Pictures come in the number you asked for.
  5. Oct 10, 2026

    0.1.1: the first run sets you up.

    • The first time you open MAT, it opens its window and asks you to connect a model; Open Providers takes you straight there.
    • What you send before a model is connected waits in the queue and goes out as soon as one is.
    • A new key works as soon as you save it, with no restart. ⌘, opens MAT’s settings.
    • Lock and encryption are off for now. Anything you encrypted in 0.1.0 is unlocked the first time 0.1.1 starts.
  6. Oct 10, 2026

    The first beta you can download.

    • MAT 0.1.0 is out as a release on GitHub: one zip for macOS 13 or later, on Apple silicon and Intel.
    • A new icon, and the app is called MAT everywhere: in Applications, the Dock and Spotlight.
  7. Oct 7, 2026

    Chats side by side, a queue, projects.

    • Chats run at the same time: two jobs in two chats no longer wait for each other.
    • What you send while MAT is busy waits above the input, up to ten. Steer one into the work now, edit it with the full input, drag to reorder, or delete it.
    • Projects: a name, instructions and files that MAT and every seat in the crew read. Chats can be pinned, renamed and moved into a project.
    • Minimised, the line under the notch has a segment per chat, and the list says what is happening in each one.
    • Memory is written as soon as a job is delivered clean, and MAT says so in the chat. Settings › Memory and Archive were rewritten.
    • Research seats search with MAT’s own browser when you have no search key, and read the top pages, not just their snippets.
  8. Oct 6, 2026

    One chat, and MAT decides.

    • Every chat is you and MAT. Each message is answered, asked about, done on one model or given a crew — and a plan starts by itself.
    • The crew works in a drawer beside the chat; with the drawer closed, one line under the chat’s title says who is doing what.
    • Stop lives in the input. Running is blue everywhere, as in the dock.
  9. Oct 3, 2026

    A ranking for every kind of work.

    • 37 models ranked in nine categories, the way Arena and Artificial Analysis score them. MAT staffs every seat from the first six.

C·01About

One chat. A crew when it pays.

MAT is a desktop app that turns the AIs you already pay for into one team.

What it is

You talk to MAT. A question gets an answer; a job gets one model, or — when the work splits — a crew of models, each on the step it does best, with MAT planning the work and checking every stage before the next one starts.

What we believe

Process beats raw IQ. A good team of ordinary models, planned and checked, does better work for less than one strong model alone — on the jobs that split. On the ones that don’t, MAT says so and uses one model.

Where it runs

On your own computer. Your chats, projects and what MAT learns stay there, and your subscriptions stay yours: MAT doesn’t resell a model, it uses the ones you connect.

C·02Privacy

What stays, what goes.

The short version, for the beta. The full policy comes with the first public build.

On your computer
Your chats, projects and their files, the queue, and MAT’s memory. Finished work is saved in Documents › MAT. Deleting a chat or a project never touches the memory; only Settings › Memory does.
Your keys
Kept on your computer, in a file only your account can read, with a copy in your keychain. Used only to call the provider they belong to.
What leaves
Each step’s work goes to the model on that seat — to the provider you connected, under your account and that provider’s terms. A seat sees the work for its step, not your whole history.
Tools
A tool you connect gets only what a seat sends it, under that service’s terms. Tools on your computer run in a sandbox, away from your keys and the files you didn’t give them. Each call is logged on your computer, nowhere else.
GitHub
The beta downloads from GitHub, and Discussions and issues live there. What you post is public and follows GitHub’s terms; we see only what you post.
This page
No cookies and no trackers. A click on Download adds one to a public counter, and nothing else is sent. GitHub counts downloads of the file itself.
